When you first look into your own horoscope, you usually know only your Sun sign. That is the zodiac sign the Sun was in on the day you were born, the one people mean when they say "star sign". Before long a second term turns up: the ascendant. In astrology it is regarded as a central point in the birth chart. Unlike the Sun sign, however, it cannot be read from the date alone. This article explains what the ascendant is, how it is calculated and why the time of birth carries so much weight.

## What is the ascendant

First, two basic terms. The zodiac is an imaginary band in the sky along which the Sun, Moon and planets appear to move when seen from Earth. It is divided into twelve equal sections of 30 degrees each, the zodiac signs: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces.

The ascendant, also called the rising sign, is the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment and place of your birth. The word comes from the Latin "ascendere", to rise. Strictly speaking, the ascendant is a point with an exact degree, for example 17 degrees Cancer. In everyday use people usually just name the sign.

The birth chart, known in astrology as the natal chart or radix, is a snapshot of the sky for exactly that moment and place. The ascendant is its starting point: on the usual circular chart it sits on the left of the horizontal line, and the whole chart is built from there.

## What the ascendant means in interpretation

Astrology is a system of interpretation. It describes themes and possibilities in a symbolic language. It does not predict events and it is not a proven science. Within this system, the ascendant has a clear role.

It stands for the way you approach the world. This covers how you come across and the first impression others form of you, as well as the attitude with which you meet new situations. The Sun sign describes something closer to your core, the things that drive you. The ascendant describes the doorway through which that core becomes visible to others.

An example: two people with the Sun in Capricorn can come across very differently. With a Leo ascendant, the Capricorn appears more open and present. With a Virgo ascendant, more reserved and precise. This is why many people say they only partly recognise themselves in their Sun sign. The ascendant often supplies the missing piece.

The first house also begins at the ascendant. In astrology, houses are twelve sections of the birth chart, each standing for an area of life such as relationships, work or home. Without the ascendant, the houses cannot be fixed. You can read more about this in the article on the twelve houses.

## How the ascendant is calculated

The calculation needs three pieces of information:

- **Date of birth**
- **Time of birth**, ideally to the minute
- **Place of birth**, which gives the geographical longitude and latitude

From these details, the sidereal time for the place of birth is worked out first. Sidereal time describes which part of the sky is overhead at that location. Together with the latitude, it shows which degree of the zodiac lies on the eastern horizon at that moment. That degree is the ascendant.

You do not need to do this calculation yourself. What matters is that the details you enter are correct.

### Local time and summer time

A common worry concerns the clock time. Do you need to convert your birth time, for example because of summer time? No. You enter the birth time as it was locally at the time, exactly as it appears on the certificate or in the birth register. The conversion to Universal Time, which the astronomical calculation requires, is done using the official time zone database. This database also records when summer time applied in which place. These rules have changed several times over the years, so a database of this kind is more reliable than converting by hand.

## Why the birth time matters so much

The Earth turns once on its axis in roughly 24 hours. In that time, the whole zodiac passes once over the eastern horizon. With twelve signs, the ascendant therefore changes sign about every two hours on average.

That average is misleading, though. Depending on the sign and the latitude, some signs rise much faster than others. At middle latitudes such as those of the UK or Germany, one sign can pass in well under an hour while another takes more than three. How long a particular ascendant lasts depends on where you are and which sign is rising.

### A few minutes can decide

If the ascendant falls in the middle of a sign, a difference of a few minutes will not change the sign. But if its degree is close to the boundary between two signs, say at 29 degrees Gemini, even a small inaccuracy can mean the ascendant is actually in Cancer. The same applies to the house cusps, the starting points of the twelve houses. They also shift with the clock time and can slip into another sign within a few minutes.

That is why it is worth knowing your birth time as precisely as possible. A rounded figure such as "around midday" is quite enough for the Sun sign, but often not for the ascendant.

## Where to find your birth time

Many people do not know their exact time of birth. There are several ways to find it:

- **Registry office of your place of birth:** The time of birth is usually recorded by the registry office where the birth was registered. In Germany this is the Standesamt, which keeps the birth register. Some birth certificates do not show the time. In that case you can request a full extract from the register from the same office.
- **Parents:** They often remember the time or wrote it down.
- **Hospital records:** The birth notes from the hospital or maternity unit usually record the time of birth.
- **Family records:** Some families keep certificates and documents that include the time.

If the time still cannot be established, a horoscope is possible all the same. Some statements, however, cannot be determined with certainty. The article on horoscopes with an unknown birth time explains what can still be calculated reliably in that case.

## Common misunderstandings

**The ascendant is not a second star sign.** It is a calculated point in the birth chart that depends on time and place. The Sun sign, by contrast, depends only on the date.

**The ascendant does not change over the course of your life.** It refers to the moment of birth and stays the same in the birth chart.

**No ascendant is better than another.** In interpretation, each sign describes its own way of approaching the world, with its own strengths and tensions.
