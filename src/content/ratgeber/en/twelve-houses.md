When you look at a birth chart, you see a circle divided into twelve sections. These sections are the houses. They are among the parts of a horoscope that most often confuse beginners, because they are easily mixed up with the zodiac signs. Yet the basic idea is simple. This article explains what houses are, what each one stands for and why there are several ways to calculate them.

## What a house is in astrology

First, a distinction. The zodiac signs, from Aries to Pisces, are twelve sections of 30 degrees each along the apparent path of the Sun across the sky. They are the same for everyone. The houses, on the other hand, are twelve sections of your personal birth chart. The birth chart, also called a natal chart or radix, is a snapshot of the sky for the moment and place of your birth.

In interpretation, each house stands for an area of life, such as relationships, work or home. Astrology is a system of interpretation. It describes themes in a symbolic language, it does not predict events and it is not a proven science.

A simple rule of thumb helps when reading a chart:

- **Planets** show which drive or need is involved.
- **Zodiac signs** show the manner in which it is expressed.
- **Houses** show the area of life in which it mainly appears.

Take Venus, which in astrology stands for relationships and appreciation, placed in Taurus in the seventh house. You would read it like this: relationships and appreciation (Venus), in a steady, sensual way (Taurus), in the area of partnership (seventh house).

## Where the houses begin

The houses begin at the ascendant. The ascendant, also called the rising sign, is the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment and place of birth. On the usual chart it sits on the left of the horizontal line. From there, the houses are counted anticlockwise, from the first to the twelfth.

The starting point of a house is called the house cusp. Because the ascendant moves into a new sign about every two hours on average as the Earth turns, all the house cusps also shift with the time of day. This is why the houses need a birth time that is as accurate as possible. The article [how to calculate your ascendant](/en/guide/ascendant) explains why even a few minutes can count.

## The four axes

Four points form the framework of the chart. They lie on two lines, known as axes.

### Ascendant and descendant

The horizontal axis corresponds to the horizon. On the left is the ascendant, the start of the first house. Directly opposite, on the right, is the descendant, the start of the seventh house. In interpretation, this axis describes the relationship between you and others: the ascendant stands for how you come across, the descendant for the other person, meaning partnerships and close relationships.

### IC and MC

The vertical axis connects the lowest and the highest point. At the top is the MC, from the Latin "Medium Coeli", the Midheaven. It is the point of the zodiac that stood highest above the horizon at birth. At the bottom is the IC, "Imum Coeli", the lowest point of the sky. This axis describes the tension between origins and public role: the IC stands for roots and home, the MC for career and what you stand for in the outside world. In most house systems, the fourth house begins at the IC and the tenth at the MC.

## The twelve houses at a glance

The following themes are standard in astrology. Each school places its own emphasis, but the core is largely the same.

**1st house: self and presence.** How you approach the world, the first impression you make, your sense of your body and your temperament.

**2nd house: possessions and values.** Material security, your own abilities as a resource and what matters to you.

**3rd house: communication and surroundings.** Speaking, everyday learning, your immediate neighbourhood and siblings.

**4th house: origins and home.** Family, roots, your own home and a sense of security.

**5th house: creativity and joy.** Play, self-expression, romance and children.

**6th house: daily life and work.** Daily routines, duties, habits and the way you work.

**7th house: partnership and the other.** Committed relationships, contracts and people who meet you as equals.

**8th house: shared matters and depth.** Shared resources, obligations, trust and deeper emotional themes.

**9th house: worldview and horizons.** Beliefs, education, travel and the search for meaning.

**10th house: career and public role.** Career path, reputation, responsibility and goals that are visible to others.

**11th house: friendships and groups.** Networks, community and goals you share with others.

**12th house: retreat and the unconscious.** Stillness, reflection and themes that tend to work below the surface.

An empty house, one without any planets, is not a bad sign, by the way. Astrology usually considers ten celestial bodies: the Sun, the Moon and eight planets. With twelve houses, some will inevitably be empty. The area of life is then interpreted through the sign on the house cusp.

## What a house system is

A house system is the method of calculation used to divide the circle into twelve houses. All systems begin at the ascendant or at the sign of the ascendant, but they draw the boundaries in between differently. This is why a planet can fall in one of two neighbouring houses, depending on the system.

### Placidus

Placidus is a common and widely used house system. It divides the houses according to the time a point in the sky takes to travel above and below the horizon. As a result, the houses are usually of different sizes. The MC forms the cusp of the tenth house.

### Koch

The Koch system is also time based but calculates in a different way. Here too the houses vary in size, and the MC sits on the cusp of the tenth house, as in Placidus. In practice, the house cusps usually differ from Placidus by only a few degrees.

### Whole sign houses

In whole sign houses, each house is exactly one whole zodiac sign. The sign containing the ascendant forms the first house, the following sign the second, and so on. The system is very old and easy to follow. Here the MC does not always fall at the start of the tenth house.

None of these systems is right or wrong. They are different ways of ordering the same sky. What matters is that it is clear which system is being used.

## And without a birth time

Without the time, the ascendant cannot be determined, and so neither can any of the houses. The Sun, the planets and their signs, on the other hand, remain largely certain. A horoscope is still possible, but statements about areas of life have to stay open.
